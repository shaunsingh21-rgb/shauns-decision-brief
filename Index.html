5 things that need attention

1. More than 300,000 visas revoked: know what that number means

What happened: On October 20, the State Department said more than 300,000 U.S. visas have been revoked since January 2025. Reuters reported that the administration cites security, legal violations and fraud; civil-liberties advocates question the breadth and fairness of some revocations.

Why it matters: South Asian families, international students, visitors, and relatives may confuse a revoked entry visa with an immediate loss of all lawful status. The consequences depend on the individual’s circumstances. A visa is a travel document; admission, authorized stay, and immigration status involve separate rules.

What to do next: If someone reports a revoked visa, collect the official notice and I-94 or status records, and arrange authorized legal review. Do not promise that a person can safely travel, reenter, or remain based on the headline alone.

2. Ukraine and Sudan TPS: protection and work authorization continue for now

What happened: USCIS country-page notices reported on October 19 say that covered Ukrainians and Sudanese retain TPS protection, including work authorization, while the government prepares further announcements. A final duration and complete employer documentation guidance remain unresolved.

Why it matters: People with an EAD showing October 19 may fear losing their jobs. The protection is meaningful, but individual I-9 documents and category details still matter.

What to do next: Save the country-specific USCIS alert, check EAD category and notices, and send difficult employer questions to qualified immigration counsel. Do not tell all workers to resign or all employers to disregard document requirements.

3. Illinois healthcare window: useful help now, possible premium shock in January

What happened: Illinois has a special marketplace enrollment period through December 31 for certain noncitizens who lost Medicaid on October 1. Qualified families can seek coverage effective as early as October 1. Financial-assistance eligibility narrows for many groups on January 1, 2027.

Why it matters: A family can lose health coverage without losing immigration status. A short-term replacement plan may be possible, but its 2027 premium could be unaffordable.

What to do next: Have staff ask for the Medicaid notice and urgent medication needs. Refer enrollment to a certified navigator at 1-866-311-1119. Review January premiums and refer any public-charge concern to authorized counsel.

Immigration and refugee lane check

What SAFC and Immigrant Hope may directly help with

General education, organizing notices, collecting dates and documents, family emergency contacts, scam warnings, language access, and benefits referrals. Immigration legal services are permitted only within the organization’s actual DOJ recognition and each representative’s accreditation.

What needs an attorney or appropriately accredited DOJ representative

Case-specific visa revocation, TPS eligibility and EAD questions, asylum, refugee adjustment, family petitions, naturalization, green-card adjustment, public charge, RFEs, electronic filing choices, immigration court, detention, and removal defense. Court representation requires the appropriate authorization.

What needs a trusted specialist referral

Marketplace and Medicaid appeals, emergency healthcare, complex detention, overseas refugee processing, unaccompanied children, trauma care, and specialized employment immigration.

Refugee status: keep the categories separate

An admissions pause can delay refugees overseas. A resettlement funding cut can reduce housing, employment help, and local case management. A benefits change can end coverage or support. None of these automatically cancels an already-admitted refugee’s status.

A formal Notice of Intent to Terminate refugee status is different and should be escalated immediately. The October 14 rule shortened the ordinary response window from thirty to ten days; counsel must calculate the actual deadline. Do not confuse refugee status with TPS or with a visa.
